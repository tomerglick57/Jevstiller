## What it looks like

Every message goes to Jev at first. As Jev's answers come in, a student is trained, checked and promoted, and the
share Jevstiller answers itself climbs, while agreement with Jev stays above the target. Local answers take
~16 ms at the median, against Jev's ~300 ms, and accuracy on the dataset's own labels matched Jev's (78.5%).

## Why

Jev is fast, cheap and typed. It is also a ceiling: **1,200 requests a minute** per key, ~300 ms per answer,
hosted only. Jevstiller is for the workload that outgrows that: bursts, backlogs, latency budgets in
milliseconds, or not wanting every classification to depend on one external API.

<Aside type="caution" title="Agreement with Jev is not accuracy">
If Jev is wrong, the student is wrong the same way. Every number Jevstiller reports is agreement with the
teacher, and the status report says so next to it.
</Aside>

## Status

**0.3.2, alpha.** The drop-in proxy works end to end with the unmodified TypeSafe SDK. It has been validated
against live Jev, tested under failure (Jev down, slow or rate limiting, a full disk, `kill -9` during training),
load (256 concurrent callers) and a silent change in Jev's answers, and audited twice. See the
[roadmap](/project/roadmap/) for what's next.
